Alienation

Galaxies apart? Not on this app. A world-building game where family members create their own characters, earn coins, and grow a shared planet that keeps them connected.

Role Product Manager, Product Designer
Duration 6 weeks
Tools Figma, Claude Code
Team Sonali Chandy & Eve Fan
Skills
✨ AI-Assisted Prototyping 🧭 Problem Framing 🔍 UX Research 🗺️ Product Strategy

01Overview

Keeping families close when they can't be in the same room

Families who live apart don't fall out of touch on purpose. The group chat goes quiet for a week, nobody is free at the same hour, and the video call slowly turns into something you schedule instead of something you look forward to.

So we built Alienation, a daily family check-in hiding inside a game. Everyone designs their own alien, and all of those aliens live together on one shared planet. Each day there is one small thing to do: dress your alien for today's theme. Nobody gets to see how it turned out until the last person has played.

02Interviews & Research

We started with families where one parent is always travelling

To understand this family dynamic better, we spoke to 8 people across 3 groups: children who grew up with a parent on the road, travelling parents, and the stationary parents who stayed home with them.

We went in assuming that families where one parent travels a lot would want more time together and miss the family bond. Both groups corrected us, but they did it in completely different ways.

The parents

Blunt, and completely logical once we sat with it. They talked about logistics and load.

"Looking back, it was taxing and tiring to call. At the end of the day you just want to wind down and sleep." Michelle, travelling parent
"When my husband came back from his travels, yes we would miss him, but it always felt like it messed up the routine we had going on." Sarah, stationary parent

The children

Something different entirely. They talked about absence, and about waiting for the weekend.

"When my body started changing during puberty, I didn't know who to talk to; I couldn't really talk to my mom about it." Jackson, child of a travelling parent
"My mom was the 'disciplining' parent since she was home, so my dad was kind of the neutralizer, and I would always look forward to him returning." Abinash, child of a travelling parent

Synthesis

After the interviews we put every quote on a board and moved them around until we discovered common themes. We used this to aid our ideation process further and look for gaps which we could fill.

Our assumptions vs. the realities

These themes also revealed a valuable contrast with our initial assumptions, which opened up our ideation even further.

Competitor Research

We also looked at what other products existed in the market and what families already keep on their phones. Many options surrounded logistical planning, things like location tracking or shared calendars. We wanted to think out of the box on what we could create in this saturated space.

Family trackers & alerts
There are dozens of them. They can tell you where somebody is, but never how they are doing.
Family calendars & organizers
A crowded category, and one more chore for whichever parent is already carrying the schedule.
Niche help-finding apps
More need-basis and only used by parents who do not have access to immediate help.

How Might We

create a low-barrier, playful shared experience that bridges the emotional gap between traveling and stationary family members?

03Ideation

Brainstorming

We explored a wide range of topics, covering everything from calendars to messaging systems to parental controls, but felt that everything touched too closely on existing products.

App Components

Landing on our idea

After looking at our competitors and initial brainstorming, we decided we wanted to make a fun, thematic alternative to what already existed. We knew these were some of the features we wanted to include:

A unique base environment. A place where everyone has some sort of character and exists together, rather than a feed to scroll or a thread to answer.
Minimal time spent, maximum effectiveness. If you have a minute, you log in, do your bit and go. We realized that different family members have different time commitments.
Combined effort for the family. Nothing moves unless everyone contributes, so the small things each person does add up into something the family owns.

Feature Prioritization

We also brainstormed features and categorized them into must, should, could and won't. After visualizing our features, we realized that we were shifting towards a game-like product instead of a utility app.

04Prototyping & User Testing

Initial Prototypes

Since we wanted something cute and thematic to keep the whole thing fun, we landed on a space theme with alien characters living on a shared planet. We started by drawing out our ideas, then proceeded to lo-fi mockups with Figma. We used these to test and get feedback on the flow through an iterative process.

Iterative User Testing

We wanted to get through as many rounds of feedback as we could with our lo-fi Figma prototypes, so we went through 5 rounds of iterative user testing to figure out the user flow.

05High-Fidelity Design

Designing with AI

Finding the vibe

For the look and feel of the app, we prompted Claude that we wanted a dark, space galaxy theme.

After the first round, the legibility was poor and the design felt a little flat, so we asked for more pink to pop out and really tie into the space pop theme. Each pass taught us to name the exact change we wanted instead of describing a vibe.

Character Design

We also needed a base character that everyone could customize, so we turned to Claude again, starting from references and correcting the shape across a few rounds until it felt like ours.

01 · What we fed in

We started by feeding Claude similar images and characters of what we were going for. Something nostalgic, but also friendly looking.

02 · The first pass

What came back first was simple. One shape, one colour each, and a single eye. We still felt the characters were too flat, and we wanted a more fluid shape.

03 · Final base character

After a few rounds of prompting, we arrived at our final base character. We asked for an onigiri-like shape so it would have a silhouette of its own.

Core Mechanics

After we got the theme and characters right, we then proceeded to convert all of the wireframes, iterating continuously until every screen matched the vibe, and fixing bugs that came up during our sessions.

06Outcomes & Reflection

Why the design works

Family apps are oversaturated with calendars and trackers, so going the other way made ours playful and low-pressure. On the planet, nobody is the person who is away, and that reaches past families to friend groups and long-distance couples.

Looking back, vibe coding the hi-fi got us to something clickable far faster than we could have built by hand, so we were testing a real interaction instead of a static flow. Prototyping in Figma first was what made that possible. The wireframes became a clear blueprint we handed to Claude Code, so every prompt could point at a concrete screen and state instead of describing one from scratch. This made our entire iterative testing and feedback process much easier.
